Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Catalyst manufacturing costs and environmental screening across traceable price states,” for publication as an Article.

The manuscript examines how manufacturing assumptions and the treatment of price evidence affect catalyst screening. COMET independently implements the published Step Method and links frozen price observations to explicit manufacturing routes, environmental-coverage statements and decision profiles. Its research contribution is an inspectable separation of numerical price effects from source-confidence scoring, accompanied by fixed-composition route/scale scenarios and area-based electrode examples.

The controlled comparison shows why a change in a recommended catalyst cannot automatically be interpreted as a market-price effect. The scale scenarios expose discontinuities introduced by discrete manufacturing classes, and the electrode examples preserve the difference between catalog material stacks and complete industrial manufacturing. Reproducible scripts, input hashes, machine-readable results and claim-to-key references accompany the analysis.

We distinguish published-method reproduction from independent manufacturing-cost validation. The public evidence audit has not yielded condition-matched industrial full-cost observations, and the manuscript does not claim an empirical prediction-error bound, validated catalyst activity or complete life-cycle superiority. Automated software checks are not presented as an external user study.
