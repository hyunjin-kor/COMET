# COMET: Catalyst Overall Manufacturing Estimation Tool

Authors, affiliations and corresponding-author contact: [to be supplied by the authors].

## Abstract

Early-stage catalyst cost estimates depend on operating inputs that preparation reports seldom state and on metal prices that change over time. COMET (Catalyst Overall Manufacturing Estimation Tool) estimates manufacturing cost from source-traceable preparation records, declared batch inputs, and dated prices for 116<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidates --> formulations in 30<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.families --> reaction families. It reproduces three published Step Method estimates within 6.65<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].residual_pct -->%. Order size changes the price of a Ni/Al₂O₃ catalyst 9.1-fold, ruthenium loading dominates that of Ru/Al₂O₃, and treatment duration and recovered mass set laboratory batch costs. Across 92<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.months --> monthly price states, the lowest-cost candidate changes in 6<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.cost_winner --> of 30<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.families --> families. COMET does not evaluate catalytic performance; coupling with performance-prediction models is future work.

Keywords: catalyst manufacturing cost; preparation provenance; sensitivity analysis; metal prices; software.

## Introduction

Catalyst development decisions depend on manufacturing cost as well as on activity, selectivity, and stability. In biomass-conversion design reports summarized by Baddour et al., catalyst cost accounted for 3–9% of installed equipment cost and moved the minimum fuel selling price by up to ±10%, yet researchers outside catalyst companies have few resources for estimating the manufacturing cost of their materials.<sup>1</sup>

Published methods address parts of this problem. The Step Method maps a laboratory synthesis onto priced industrial processing steps at an order-size-dependent scale and margin; for three commercial catalysts, its estimates agreed with market prices within ±20%.<sup>1</sup> CatCost added dedicated-plant costing, spent-catalyst value, sensitivity analysis, and, with life-cycle assessment, a weighted comparison of catalyst candidates.<sup>2</sup> Route-specific studies have costed metal-oxide<sup>3</sup> and platinum–strontium titanate syntheses,<sup>4</sup> and BioSTEAM propagates parameter uncertainty through techno-economic analyses.<sup>5</sup>

Applying these methods to literature candidates raises three problems. First, reported conditions do not map onto cost inputs: a furnace temperature does not specify electricity use, a precursor charge does not establish recovered mass, and treatment time differs from attended labor; estimates that fill such gaps silently cannot be audited. Second, metal prices move, so a cost ordering obtained at one date may not hold at another, and quotations differ in reliability. Third, when cost is combined with other criteria, min–max normalized scores depend on which candidates are compared.<sup>6,7</sup>

COMET was developed to make these conditions explicit. It extends Step Method and CatCost accounting with source-linked preparation records, batch costing from declared operating inputs, dated price histories, and ranking diagnostics. We verify it against the published Step Method examples and show what sets the manufacturing cost of 116<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidates --> formulations in 30<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.families --> reaction families and how observed prices change their ordering.

## Implementation

### Software design

In Figure 1, a user enters a formulation, edits its route and scale, selects a price basis, and obtains an estimate listing its sources and missing inputs; saved estimates can be recalculated, compared, and ranked. A FastAPI (Python) backend stores prices and estimates in SQLite, and a bilingual React/TypeScript interface is packaged for Windows with Electron and PyInstaller; Linux backend and interface builds were also tested. Exports and analysis records keep provenance, checksums, software versions, and seeds.

### Cost models

Screening estimates follow the Step Method (Figure 2a).<sup>1</sup> Materials cost is Cₘ = Σ<sub>i</sub>w<sub>i</sub>c<sub>i</sub>, with component mass fraction w<sub>i</sub> and price c<sub>i</sub> per kg; a precursor requires $w/(f\,p\,y)$ kg per kg of catalyst (component fraction $w$, fraction in the pure precursor $f$, purity $p$, retention $y$). Processing cost is Cₚ = 24TIH/M, with campaign duration T in days, price-index factor I, summed hourly cost H of the selected operations, and catalyst mass M in kg. Order size sets scale, rate, and cleaning time; documented effective rates can replace nominal ones. Hourly rates use the published 2017 basis escalated by a producer price index; 28<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:manufacturing.20.template_count --> editable procedures are provided, with substituted or uncosted steps flagged. The selling price is P = (Cₘ + Cₚ)(1 + g)(1 + s)/(1 − m), with general and administrative (G&A) fraction g, sales, administrative, research, and distribution (SARD) fraction s, and margin m from the published order-size correlation. Cost denotes P per kilogram.

For a laboratory preparation, batch costing replaces these terms with purchases and operating inputs (Supporting Information, eqs S1–S6): electricity from measured kWh or mean power over ramp and hold times, equipment occupancy, attended labor, gases, and transfers allocated by recovered mass or solution volume. Costs are divided by the dry output mass before overheads and margin, and a missing required input stops the calculation.

Gray includes G&A, SARD, margin, and assumed route allowances for quality assurance, activation, and additional overhead. (c) Deviations from market prices reported by Baddour et al.<sup>1</sup>; negative values denote lower estimates. SCR, selective catalytic reduction; RWGS, reverse water–gas shift. ### Preparation records

Preparation records trace these inputs to their sources. An audit of the 116<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:candidates --> candidates verified 401<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:crossref_dois --> digital object identifiers (DOIs) with Crossref and transcribed 92<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:profiles --> specimen-specific records from 71<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:primary_sources --> sources, linked to 75<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:candidates_with_profile --> candidates; 26<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:source_mismatches --> candidates carry composition or source discrepancies. Each value keeps its DOI, locator, and original value; user edits are stored separately, and unreported conditions stay blank. A linked specimen does not validate the catalog formulation (Supporting Information, Section S6).

### Prices and ranking

Each price keeps its source, date, and retrieval time. Current quotations include Johnson Matthey platinum and palladium<sup>8</sup> and Westmetall copper and aluminum.<sup>9</sup> Reference prices are monthly averages from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) Primary Commodity Price System<sup>10</sup> and Johnson Matthey; support prices are U.S. import unit values,<sup>11</sup> which are published later and enter at their latest verified month. Results use August 2026<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:basis_month --> reference prices. A price-source grade, weighted by each price's share of materials cost, rates provenance only. Environmental screening uses cradle-to-gate metal factors.<sup>12</sup>

The library comprises 83<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:screening_basis_counts.literature_architecture_proxy --> literature-architecture proxies, 29<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:screening_basis_counts.engineering_proxy --> engineering proxies, and 4 candidates with documented vendor or market bases. Rankings combine cost, price-data reliability, and route and performance scores assigned from the literature; within each family, cost scores run from 100 (least expensive) to 0 (most expensive), and families lacking electrode data are compared on powder cost.

### Recommendation sensitivity

A cost crossover need not change a ranking. The balanced leader of ammonia cracking stays Co/MgO–La₂O₃ in all 92<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:families[1].periods.monthly.summary.winner_counts.app_winner.co-mgo-la2o3 --> states: the far costlier Ru/MgO (2,925.80<!-- methods-2026-09-21/methods_study.json:normalization.example.removed_cost --> USD/kg) fixes the top of the cost scale, so the two cost leaders keep cost scores of at least 99.7. Removing Ru/MgO contracts that scale, magnifies the 1.17 USD/kg difference between the two, and reverses their ranking (89.4<!-- methods-2026-09-21/methods_study.json:normalization.example.rows[0].total_before --> versus 87.4<!-- methods-2026-09-21/methods_study.json:normalization.example.rows[2].total_before --> before; 72.2<!-- methods-2026-09-21/methods_study.json:normalization.example.rows[0].total_after --> versus 87.4<!-- methods-2026-09-21/methods_study.json:normalization.example.rows[2].total_after --> after); retaining the original range prevents this.<sup>6,7</sup> Candidate removal changes the leader in 8<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidate_removal_winner_changes --> of 86<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidate_removal_cases --> tests, and replacing the reference prices with live quotations collected on September 21, 2026<!-- submission-2026-09-21/live_basis_2026-09-21.json:observation_started_at_utc --> changes it in 4<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:live_reference_comparison.changed_by_profile.balanced --> of 30<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.families --> families, partly because metals lacking them, such as cobalt, fall back to less reliable annual prices (Figure S6; Table S8). Rankings thus need their candidate set, price basis, and date.

### Limitations

COMET estimates manufacturing cost only, not activity, selectivity, or lifetime, so a more expensive catalyst can still be the more economical choice per unit of product; the route and performance scores in rankings are literature-based screening judgments, not measured or predicted activity. Industrial accuracy remains unvalidated because no observations matched formulation, grade, scale, date, and boundary; mean absolute percentage error was not calculated. Environmental coverage averages 62.79<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:lca.coverage_mean_pct -->% of catalyst mass (47<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:lca.candidates_coverage_below_50_pct --> candidates below 50%) and excludes solvents, wastewater treatment, and equipment manufacture.

## Conclusions

COMET makes explicit the conditions behind early-stage catalyst cost estimates: input sources, price dates, and the candidates behind a ranking. It reproduces the published Step Method examples within 6.65<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].residual_pct -->%. Order size and procedure set the price of base-metal catalysts, metal loading that of precious-metal catalysts, and treatment duration and recovered mass, which publications seldom state, that of a laboratory batch. Observed prices changed the lowest-cost candidate in 6<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.cost_winner --> of 30<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.families --> families. Cost comparisons should therefore report price basis, date, candidate set, and assumed inputs. Because COMET does not evaluate performance, we plan to couple it with catalyst performance-prediction models so that cost per unit of product can be compared, and to validate it against condition-matched industrial prices; synthesis optimization<sup>13</sup> and catalyst lifetime<sup>14</sup> are related extensions.
