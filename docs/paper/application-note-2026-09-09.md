# COMET: Catalyst Overall Manufacturing Estimation Tool

Authors, affiliations and corresponding-author contact: [to be supplied by the authors].

## Abstract

Early-stage catalyst cost estimates depend on operating inputs that synthesis reports seldom state and on metal prices that change, yet estimates rarely record either. COMET is desktop software that implements the Step Method, a published procedure that prices a synthesis as a sequence of industrial processing steps, and adds source-linked preparation records, batch costing from declared operating inputs, dated price histories, and ranking sensitivity tests. It reproduces the method's three published examples line by line; the largest price difference, 6.65<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].residual_pct -->%, arises from the margin rule. Order size changes the price of Ni/Al₂O₃ 9.1-fold, metal loading controls that of Ru/Al₂O₃, and recovered mass and treatment duration control laboratory batch cost. Over 92<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.months --> months of metal prices, the lowest-cost candidate changes in 6<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.cost_winner --> of 30<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.families --> reaction families. COMET estimates cost, not performance; coupling with performance-prediction models is planned.

Keywords: catalyst manufacturing cost; preparation provenance; sensitivity analysis; metal prices; software.

## Introduction

Catalyst manufacturing cost affects process economics alongside activity, selectivity, and stability. In biomass-conversion design reports, it amounted to 3–9% of installed equipment cost and shifted the minimum fuel selling price by up to ±10%, yet researchers outside catalyst companies have few resources for estimating it.<sup>1</sup>

Two published tools address this need. The Step Method estimates the purchase price of a precommercial catalyst by mapping its laboratory synthesis onto industrial processing steps with hourly costs and adding materials, overheads, and an order-size-dependent margin; for three commercial catalysts, its estimates fell within ±20% of market prices.<sup>1</sup> CatCost built the method into spreadsheet and web tools and added dedicated-plant costing, spent-catalyst value, sensitivity analysis, and, with life-cycle assessment, a weighted comparison of candidates.<sup>2</sup> Other studies costed individual synthesis routes<sup>3,4</sup> or propagated input uncertainty through techno-economic models.<sup>5</sup>

These tools take their inputs as given, which leaves three problems when candidates come from the literature. First, the inputs behind processing cost are seldom reported: a furnace temperature does not give electricity use, nor a precursor charge the recovered mass, and an estimate that fills such gaps silently cannot be audited. Second, metal prices move, so a cost order obtained on one date need not hold on another. Third, when cost is combined with other criteria, min–max normalized scores depend on the candidates included, so removing one candidate can reorder the others.<sup>6,7</sup>

COMET records these conditions with every estimate. It implements the Step Method and adds source-linked preparation records and batch costing from declared inputs (first problem), dated price histories (second), and ranking sensitivity tests (third). We ask whether the implementation reproduces the published method, which inputs set the estimated cost at laboratory and production scale, and how often observed metal prices change the cost order and the recommended candidate.

## Implementation

### Software design

COMET is a desktop application (Figure 1). A user enters a formulation, selects a preparation procedure and order size, chooses a price basis, and obtains an estimate listing its sources, assumed inputs, and missing inputs; saved estimates can be repriced, compared, and ranked. A Python backend (FastAPI, SQLite) serves a React/TypeScript interface in English and Korean, packaged for Windows with Electron.

### Cost models

COMET has two cost models. Production-scale estimates follow the Step Method (Figure 2a).<sup>1</sup> Materials cost is Cₘ = Σ<sub>i</sub>w<sub>i</sub>c<sub>i</sub>, with mass fraction w<sub>i</sub> and price c<sub>i</sub> of component i. Processing cost is Cₚ = 24TIH/M, where H is the summed hourly cost of the selected processing steps (2017 basis), I a producer-price-index factor, T the campaign duration in days, and M the order mass in kg; order size sets the production scale, step rates, and T. The selling price is P = (Cₘ + Cₚ)(1 + g)(1 + s)/(1 − m), with general and administrative (G&A) fraction g, sales, administrative, research, and distribution (SARD) fraction s, and margin m from the published order-size correlation. P is the price a buyer would pay, and cost below means P per kilogram. COMET provides 28<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:manufacturing.20.template_count --> editable procedures and flags substituted or uncosted steps.

Laboratory batch costing replaces these terms with declared purchases and operating inputs: electricity, equipment occupancy, attended labor, gases, and transfers between operations (Supporting Information, eqs S1–S6). The sum is divided by the recovered dry mass before overheads and margin are applied, and a missing required input stops the calculation instead of taking a default.

### Library and preparation records

The library holds 116<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidates --> formulations in 30<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.families --> reaction families (23 of powder catalysts, 7 of electrocatalysts), each with a composition, procedure, order size, and route and performance scores. Compositions follow a literature catalyst architecture for 83<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:screening_basis_counts.literature_architecture_proxy --> candidates, engineering baselines for 29<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:screening_basis_counts.engineering_proxy -->, and vendor or market data for 4; all are screening models, not verified recipes, and their estimates add assumed route allowances (Figure 2c; Table S7). Preparation records link candidates to what their sources report: an audit verified 401<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:crossref_dois --> digital object identifiers (DOIs) with Crossref and transcribed 92<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:profiles --> specimen-specific records from 71<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:primary_sources --> primary sources for 75<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:candidates_with_profile --> candidates, flagging composition or source discrepancies in 26<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:source_mismatches -->. Each value keeps its DOI and locator, and unreported conditions stay blank. A record documents a source; it does not validate the library formulation (Section S6).

### Prices and ranking

Prices are stored with source, date, and retrieval time. The reference basis uses monthly averages from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) Primary Commodity Price System<sup>10</sup> and Johnson Matthey<sup>8</sup> for metals and U.S. import unit values for supports<sup>11</sup> (Section S5). Results use the August 2026<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:basis_month --> reference basis, and the same series give 92<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.months --> monthly price states from January 2019<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.first_month -->. The live basis uses current quotations, for example from Johnson Matthey<sup>8</sup> and Westmetall.<sup>9</sup> A price-reliability score averages source grades by share of materials cost; it rates price provenance, not estimate accuracy.

Within a family, candidates are ranked by a weighted sum of four 0–100 scores: cost, price reliability, route, and performance. The cost score is min–max normalized from 100 (least expensive) to 0 (most expensive); route and performance scores are assigned from the literature. The balanced weight profile used below gives cost the largest weight (0.40–0.45).

## Results and Discussion

### Verification

The source of the Step Method tabulates three demonstration cases (Pt/C, Ni/Al₂O₃, and a fluid catalytic cracking (FCC) catalyst) with every intermediate line and a market price.<sup>1</sup> Their inputs were entered as published. For Pt/C and Ni/Al₂O₃, COMET reproduces every line before the margin within 0.4%, the rounding of the published table (Table S1), and the Pt/C price matches (60.34<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[0].comet_usd_per_lb --> USD/kg, platinum value excluded). The Ni/Al₂O₃ price is 42.37<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].comet_usd_per_lb --> against 45.39<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].published_usd_per_lb --> USD/kg (−6.65<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].residual_pct -->%), and the whole difference lies in the margin: the table applies 33<!-- submission-2026-09-21/table62_reproduction_2026-09-21.json:[1].margin.table_footnote_pct_of_premargin -->% of pre-margin cost, whereas COMET applies the order-size correlation of the same source, which gives 24<!-- submission-2026-09-21/table62_reproduction_2026-09-21.json:[1].margin.comet_pct_of_premargin -->%. For FCC, costed at the effective production rate that the source states (60,781.4<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[2].effective_rate_ton_per_day --> kg/day), COMET gives 5.37<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[2].comet_usd_per_lb --> against 5.31<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[2].published_usd_per_lb --> USD/kg (+1.16<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[2].residual_pct -->%). Against the market prices of the source, the COMET estimates deviate by −19.7<!-- submission-2026-09-21/table62_reproduction_2026-09-21.json:[0].market.comet_vs_market_pct -->%, −9.9<!-- submission-2026-09-21/table62_reproduction_2026-09-21.json:[1].market.comet_vs_market_pct -->%, and −10.7% (Figure 2b), within the ±20% reported for the method. These results verify the implementation; they do not validate new estimates, because the market comparison covers three catalysts at mid-2017 prices.

### Production-scale cost structure

Figure 2c divides the selling price of the lowest-cost candidate in each of the 23 powder-catalyst families. Materials exceed half of the price in only 3 families, processing exceeds materials in 12, and G&A, SARD, margin, and route allowances take 24–42%. Most estimates thus depend on processing assumptions as much as on materials prices. The Step Method supplies these assumptions as standard hourly step costs; for a laboratory preparation, they must come from the preparation itself.

### Laboratory batch costs

Published preparations rarely report the operating inputs that batch costing needs. In the 92<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:profiles --> preparation records, all 272 heating segments state a target temperature, 208 a hold time, and 52 a ramp rate, but none states the electrical input, and only 1 record reports the recovered batch mass. Duration outweighs power: calcination holds of 1<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[4].low -->–6<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[4].high --> h span 1,875.45<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[4].low_usd_kg -->–2,512.45<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[4].high_usd_kg --> USD/kg, whereas hold powers of 0.6<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[5].low -->–2.4<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[5].high --> kW span only 2,122.90<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[5].low_usd_kg -->–2,144.95<!-- manufacturing-study-2026-09-15/manufacturing_study.json:sensitivity[5].high_usd_kg --> USD/kg (Table S6). ### Production-scale what-if analyses

At production scale, the Step Method derives operating inputs from the procedure and order size, leaving composition, procedure, and order size to the user. Each was varied in turn for two alumina-supported catalysts prepared by incipient wetness impregnation (Figure 4a–c; Table S8). The base case, 20<!-- figures-note-2026-09-09/reference_example_ni_al2o3.json:request.components[0].wt_pct --> wt% Ni/Al₂O₃ ordered at 18,143.7<!-- figures-note-2026-09-09/reference_example_ni_al2o3.json:request.order_size_tons --> kg, sells for 10.33<!-- figures-note-2026-09-09/reference_example_ni_al2o3.json:step_method.estimated_price_per_lb --> USD/kg. Raising the nickel loading from 5<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:loading.ni[0].loading_wt_pct --> to 30<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:loading.ni[5].loading_wt_pct --> wt% raises the price from 7.02<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:loading.ni[0].selling_price_per_lb --> to 12.54<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:loading.ni[5].selling_price_per_lb --> USD/kg (0.22 USD/kg per wt%), whereas each wt% of ruthenium adds 737 USD/kg. Order size acts in the opposite direction: from 907.2<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:order_size.ni[0].order_size_tons --> to 907,184.7<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:order_size.ni[10].order_size_tons --> kg, the Ni/Al₂O₃ price falls 9.1-fold, from 46.21<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:order_size.ni[0].selling_price_per_lb --> to 5.06<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:order_size.ni[10].selling_price_per_lb --> USD/kg, because a larger order moves production to a larger scale and lowers the margin; the price of 2<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:catalysts.ru.loading_wt_pct --> wt% Ru/Al₂O₃ falls only 1.5-fold because materials dominate it. Five procedures for the same Ni/Al₂O₃ composition span 10.33<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:preparation[0].selling_price_per_lb -->–11.59<!-- whatif-2026-09-21/whatif_study.json:preparation[2].selling_price_per_lb --> USD/kg. For the base-metal catalyst, order size thus outweighs loading, and loading outweighs procedure; for the precious-metal catalyst, loading outweighs both.

### Recommendation sensitivity

A change in cost order need not change the recommendation. In ammonia cracking, the balanced-profile leader remains Co/MgO–La₂O₃ in all 92<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:families[1].periods.monthly.summary.winner_counts.app_winner.co-mgo-la2o3 --> months because the cost score is normalized within the family: the precious-metal candidate Ru/MgO, at 2,925.80<!-- methods-2026-09-21/methods_study.json:normalization.example.removed_cost --> USD/kg, fixes the expensive end of the scale, so the two cost leaders keep cost scores of at least 99.7 and the other scores decide their order. The same normalization ties the recommendation to the candidate set: removing Ru/MgO contracts the scale, magnifies the 1.17 USD/kg difference between the two, and reverses their ranking, a known property of min–max normalization;<sup>6,7</sup> retaining the original range prevents the reversal. Over all families, removing one candidate that does not rank first changes the leader in 8<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidate_removal_winner_changes --> of 86<!-- robustness-2026-09-21/decision_robustness.json:summary.candidate_removal_cases --> tests (Figure S6). The price basis matters as well: live quotations collected on September 21, 2026<!-- submission-2026-09-21/live_basis_2026-09-21.json:observation_started_at_utc --> change the leader in 4<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:live_reference_comparison.changed_by_profile.balanced --> of 30<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.families --> families, partly because metals without a live quotation, such as cobalt, fall back to annual prices with a lower reliability grade (Table S9). A recommendation should therefore carry its candidate set, price basis, and date.

### Limitations

COMET estimates manufacturing cost only, not activity, selectivity, or lifetime, so a more expensive catalyst can still be the more economical choice per unit of product;<sup>12</sup> the route and performance scores are literature-based screening judgments, not measured or predicted activity. Accuracy against industrial prices is not established, because no public price matched a library formulation in grade, scale, date, and delivery boundary.

## Conclusions

COMET implements the Step Method and records, with each estimate, its input sources, price date, and candidate set. It reproduces the three published examples line by line; the largest price difference, 6.65<!-- submission-2026-09-21/paper_summary_2026-09-21.json:table62[1].residual_pct -->%, arises from the margin rule. Order size sets the estimated cost of a base-metal catalyst, metal loading that of a precious-metal catalyst, and treatment duration and recovered mass that of a laboratory batch, although only 1 of 92<!-- manufacturing-2026-09-14/review_summary.json:profiles --> preparation records reports the recovered mass. Observed metal prices changed the lowest-cost candidate in 6<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.cost_winner --> of 30<!-- price-crossovers-2026-09-21/price_crossovers.json:summary.monthly.families --> families, and recommendations changed with the candidate set and the price basis. Cost comparisons should therefore state order size, assumed operating inputs, price basis and date, and candidate set. Because COMET does not evaluate performance, we plan to couple it with catalyst performance-prediction models, so that cost per unit of product can be compared, and to validate it against industrial prices under matched conditions.
