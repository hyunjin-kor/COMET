- Balanced-profile winner keeps rank 1 on a **median 58.0%** of the grid (min 20.6%, max 92.7%); **8 of 30 families below 50%**.

## 3. Does the recommendation survive the price basis?

Sections 1 and 2 are computed at one price snapshot. This section re-ranks every family at a series of real historical price states and asks whether the recommended catalyst is a property of the chemistry or of the day the tool was run.

**Method.** One price state per period, using each symbol's last observation in that period. Real date slices rather than independent per-metal percentiles, so co-movement between metals is preserved and every state is a set of prices that occurred together. A symbol that does not cover the whole window holds its baseline value in every state rather than being spliced in part way, which would put a step change into the series and read as market movement.

Two windows are reported because feed coverage differs. The long window is primary; the short one is the only way to vary nickel.

| | Long window | Short window |
|---|---|---|
| Source | `price_history_2026-09-02.json` (Johnson Matthey, Yahoo, Westmetall) | application database |
| Span | 2021-09-30 – 2026-09-30, **61 monthly states** | 2026-03-25 – 2026-09-02, 24 daily states |
| Varying | Ag, Al, Au, Cu, Ir, Pd, Pt, Rh, Ru | Ag, Al, Au, Cu, Ir, **Ni**, Pd, Pt, Rh, Ru |
| Held at baseline | Ni, Sn, Zn | — |

### Result: the winner changes in 4 of 30 families

Over five years, four families change their recommended catalyst on the performance-zero ranking (three on the balanced profile). All four are sustained runs, not single-month noise.

| Family | Winners (states) | Distinguishing metal | Minority run |
|---|---|---|---|
| `photocatalytic-water-splitting` | `tio2-anatase-baseline` 42 / `pt-tio2-cocatalyst` 19 | Pt 1 wt% against none | 19 consecutive months, 2021-09 – 2023-03 |
| `co2-to-formate` | `pdag-ndcarbon-co2fa` 37 / `ru-sa-ldh` 24 | Pd+Ag 5 wt% each against Ru 0.5 wt% | 24 months in 3 runs, 2021-09 – 2026-01 |
| `co-prox` | `cuo-ceo2` 52 / `pt-fe-alumina` 9 | Cu 5.6 wt% against Pt 0.5 wt% | 9 consecutive months, 2024-09 – 2025-05 |
| `nitrogen-reduction-reaction` | `li-mediated-nrr` 53 / `plasma-nrr` 8 | Cu against Ni | 8 months in 2 runs, 2026-01 – 2026-09 |

The clearest case is photocatalytic water splitting, where the two candidates are the same oxide and the only difference is a 1 wt% platinum co-catalyst. Whether that loading is worth its cost is not a fixed property of the pair: across the 19 months the Pt-loaded candidate won, platinum sat between $878 and $1069/oz against $1796 at the last state. The separation is not on platinum alone — platinum revisits $902 later in the window without the ranking returning — so the flip is the composite crossing, not a single price threshold. Either way, a screening result that names one of these two is quoting a price basis as much as the chemistry.

Nitrogen reduction carries a caveat: nickel has no long feed, so in the long window it is held at baseline and the flip is driven by copper alone. The short window, where both vary, separates cleanly and gives the threshold directly — `li-mediated-nrr` (100 wt% Cu) wins at Cu/Ni 0.685–0.763 and `plasma-nrr` (100 wt% Ni) wins at 0.830–0.890, with no overlap. Copper rose 17.6% over that window while nickel fell 2.9%. The exact crossing is unobserved because the recorded series has no data between 2026-05-04 and 2026-07-20.

### Reading

The families that stay stable are those whose candidates share a metal, or whose loadings are too small for the metal term to move the composite. The four that flip all share one property: **their candidates depend on different metals whose prices decouple** — a platinum-group metal against a base metal, one PGM against another, or a metal loading against none at all. That is the condition under which a screening ranking should not be quoted without its price basis.

Note also that the ranking is a composite, not a cost ordering. In the short window `plasma-nrr` was already the cheaper candidate at the first state ($31.81 against $33.22 per lb) yet lost on evidence and route scores; the flip is where a widening cost gap ($1.41 to $3.75) overtakes those terms, not a pure cost crossover.

### Data handling

The database replay excludes non-market rows: seeded reference values, CatCost escalation and USGS annual averages are point estimates, not observations, and 2,169 such rows were dropped. Including them inflates the answer badly — the seeded first day alone reads as Au 2000 → 4404 and Pt 950 → 1891 overnight, and replaying it makes **7 of 30** families appear to flip, six of them artefacts of that seeding.

A further 25 rows were dropped as outliers: aluminium on 2026-03-24 (22 rows quoted per metric ton, ~2000x high) and rhodium on 2026-04-03 (3 rows pinned at a 1001.0 parse failure). Both clusters are listed in the JSON. Since this run, `backend/services/price_scheduler.check_price` rejects such quotes at the write boundary, so neither failure mode can enter the table again; `backend/tests/test_price_scheduler_guard.py` pins both cases.

## 4. At what metal price does the active-metal choice flip?

> **Provisional — concept dry run on the 2026-09-02 software state.** This
> section fixes the analysis design and table layout; the numbers are to be
> regenerated after the software update and are not final results.

Sections 3 replays history and reports *whether* a recommendation moved. This section asks the forward question a screening user actually has: for a reaction where the literature offers a precious-metal catalyst and a base-metal one — Ru or Ni for ammonia cracking, Pt or Cu for CO-PROX and water-gas shift, Rh or Ni for steam reforming, Ir or Ni for dry reforming, Pt/C or NiOOH for an electro-oxidation anode — **at what price of the distinguishing metal does the cheaper option change?**

**Method.** For every family, the cost-only winner at the 2026-09-02 basis is paired with each rival that depends on a different market-fed metal. For each metal only one of the pair contains, that metal's price is swept over 0.001×–100× of today with every other price held, and the crossing is found by a log-grid scan and bisection (`backend/core/breakeven.py`). Two crossings are reported: on landed cost, and on the performance-zero MCDA composite. The five-year monthly record then gives how many months sat on each side. Families whose candidates share the same metals, or carry no market-fed metal at all, are classed as having no exposure rather than as "stable". 30 families, 120 contests, no evaluation errors (`active_metal_breakeven_2026-09-02.json`).

| Exposure class | Families | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| precious vs base metal | 15 | ammonia cracking (Ru/Ni), CO₂ methanation (Ru/Ni), dry reforming (Ir/Ni), RWGS and WGS (Pt/Cu), SMR (Rh/Ni), HDO, HER, glycerol anode (Pt/Ni) |
| metal vs none (oxide, zeolite, carbon) | 11 | MTO (SAPO-34, ZSM-5), NH₃-SCR, FCC-type, photocatalytic TiO₂, PDH (CrOx), ammonia synthesis (fused Fe) |
| base vs base | 3 | NRR (Cu/Ni), olefin metathesis (W/Mo), ethylene epoxidation (Ag/Ag-Cu) |
| precious vs precious | 1 | CO₂-to-formate (Ru/Pd/Ir) |

### Finding 1 — at literature loadings, the precious metal never competes on cost

28 sweeps move the precious metal's price against a base-metal winner.

| | Count |
|---|---:|
| cost crossing exists somewhere in 0.001×–100× | 13 / 28 |
| median crossing, as a multiple of today's price | **0.0038×** (0.4%) |
| crossing within 0.1×–10× of today | 3 / 28 |
| months in the 5-year record on the precious side | 1 / 28 contests (see Finding 3) |
| precious metal cannot win **even at zero price** | **15 / 28** |

Ru/MgO (3 wt%) matches Ni/Al₂O₃ for ammonia cracking only at Ru ≈ **$1.7/oz** (today $1,700); Ir/CeO₂ (0.6 wt%) matches Ni-zeolite for dry reforming at Ir ≈ **$9/oz**; Pt/TiO₂ (2 wt%) matches Cu/CeO₂ for RWGS at Pt ≈ **$2/oz**; Pd/ZnO (2.5 wt%) matches Cu/ZnO/Al₂O₃ for syngas-to-methanol at Pd ≈ **$10/oz**. In 15 of 28 sweeps the crossing does not exist at all: the precious-metal route's support, precursor conversion and processing already cost more than the entire base-metal catalyst, so a free metal would not close the gap. Ruthenium has not traded below $250/oz in the record.

The practical statement for a screening user: a 1–5 wt% PGM formulation against a 10–20 wt% base-metal one is a decision about activity and lifetime, not about metal price. No plausible market movement makes it a cost decision.

### Finding 2 — loading, not metal identity, sets exposure

The three sweeps that cross within 0.1×–10× are the exceptions that mark the rule:

| Family | Precious candidate | Loading | Cost break-even | vs today |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| selective acetylene hydrogenation | Pd/Al₂O₃ | **0.04 wt%** | $196/oz | 0.15× |
| glycerol electro-oxidation | Au/C anode | 20 wt% | $1,020/oz | 0.23× |
| glycerol electro-oxidation | Pt/C anode | 20 wt% | $1,020/oz | **0.58×** |

At 0.04 wt% Pd the metal term is small enough that palladium at one-seventh of today's price would make the PGM catalyst the cheaper one. The two electrode cases cross because the base-metal rival (100 wt% NiOOH on a fixed-cost route) is itself expensive per lb, not because 20 wt% Pt is cheap.

### Finding 3 — the one contest that has actually flipped: Pt/C vs NiOOH

For the glycerol electro-oxidation anode the Pt/C candidate is cheaper whenever platinum is below **$1,020/oz**. Platinum was below that in **63% of the 93 months** on record (2019-01 to 2026-09) and is above it today. This is the same family the historical replay in Section 3 did not flag, because that replay ranks on the composite: the composite break-even is $355/oz, which platinum never reached. The two views together say the anode's *cost* ordering has changed sides for most of the last seven years while its *recommendation* has not — the evidence and route scores have been carrying the Ni candidate.

### Finding 4 — plausible flips live in base-metal contests and in the composite, not in PGM cost

Cost crossings within 0.5×–2× of today, i.e. inside ordinary market movement:

| Family | Contest | Metal swept | Break-even | vs today | Months on B's side |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| ammonia cracking | Ni/Al₂O₃ vs Co/MgO-La₂O₃ | Ni | $8.07/lb | **1.09×** | 22% of 9 (2026 only) |
| ammonia cracking | Ni/Al₂O₃ vs Co/MgO-La₂O₃ | Co | $19.3/lb | **0.92×** | no Co history |
| dry reforming | Ni-zeolite vs Ni-Co/Al-Mg-O | Co | $17.5/lb | **0.83×** | no Co history |
| glycerol anode | NiOOH vs Pt/C | Pt | $1,020/oz | 0.58× | 63% of 93 |

A 9% rise in nickel, or an 8% fall in cobalt, changes the cheapest ammonia-cracking catalyst. Cobalt fell by more than half between 2022 and 2024; the analysis cannot yet count those months because cobalt, molybdenum and tungsten have no frozen history (next fetch).

On the performance-zero composite the picture shifts: 16 of the 28 precious-metal sweeps cross, at a median 0.13× and **8 of them within 0.1×–10×** (CO-PROX Pt at 0.70×, HDO Pt at 0.71×, CO₂-to-formate Pd at 0.96×, formic-acid Pt at 0.39×). This is why Section 3 finds flips that Finding 1 does not: **the recommendation's price sensitivity comes from cost interacting with the evidence and route scores, not from cost crossovers alone.** A tool that reported cost only would call these families insensitive; a tool that reported the composite only would not show that the cost ordering never moved.

### Zeolite and oxide families are the control group

MTO (SAPO-34, H-ZSM-5), NH₃-SCR (V₂O₅-WO₃/TiO₂, Cu-SSZ-13, Fe-ZSM-5), the FCC-type routes, photocatalytic TiO₂ and CrOx PDH carry no market-fed metal, or a trace of one (Cu 2.5 wt% in SSZ-13, Zn 3 wt% in ZSM-5), so their cost cannot move with a metal price: in every one of these families the metal-free candidate wins on cost at any swept price. These are the largest-tonnage solid catalysts in use — FCC alone is several hundred kilotonnes a year — so their absence from the price-sensitivity story is itself a result: the volume end of the catalyst market is insulated from metal markets, and the exposure sits in the PGM- and Ni/Co-based hydrogenation, reforming and electrochemical families. Their remaining weakness in this dataset is the LCA side, where zeolites and carbons still lack a factor.

### Limitations

Loadings and routes are literature-architecture proxies (Section 2), so a break-even is a property of the representative composition, not of any specific commercial product. Cobalt, molybdenum and tungsten have no long price series yet; nickel, zinc and tin cover 2026 only. The sweep holds every other price fixed, so co-movement (Ni and Co often move together) is not represented here — Section 3's date slices are the complement.
